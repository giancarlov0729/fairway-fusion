# PiTrac V3 driver and IRLED2 circuit walkthrough

This follow-up documents the reference design reviewed on November 10. It uses the seven-sheet PiTrac schematic whose title block reads **V3 Connector + IRLED**, Rev V1, dated February 12, 2026. The V3 Connector board generates and controls the strobe current. The IRLED2 board contains the emitters. The values below describe the reference schematic. Fairway Fusion’s assembled board, substitutions, and timing still require bench measurements. [Schematic](https://github.com/PiTracLM/PiTrac/blob/main/Hardware/Schematic/V3%20Connector%20%2B%20IRLED2%20Schematic.pdf) · [Assembly and fabrication files](https://docs.pitrac.org/hardware/pcb-assembly/)

## V3 Connector board

| Stage | Components | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power and logic connections · sheet 2 | J1, J2, J3, J4, J5, J6 | J1 receives the 5 V supply. J2 and J5 are 5 V power-only USB-C outputs in the reference design, and J6 is an optional 5 V USB-A output. J3 carries 3.3 V, ground, shared SPI clock and data, two chip selects, and the DIAG signal. J4 carries **VIR** and **VIR-RTN** to the emitters. |
| Boost converter and reservoir · sheet 3 | U1 MC33063AP, L1 33 µH, D1 SB150, R1 0.15 Ω, C4 470 pF, C5 1 µF, C6 330 µF | U1 switches L1. D1 rectifies the switch-node pulses to charge the VIR rail and its output capacitors. R1 senses converter switch current, C4 sets the oscillator timing, and the R3/R4 feedback divider sets the nominal output near 36 V. C6 is the bulk energy reservoir for the short flash. The boost stage recharges it between flashes. D2 indicates voltage on VIR. |
| Adjustable gate-drive rail · sheets 4 and 5 | U5 MCP4801, Q4 2N3904, U2 LM317L | U5 is an 8-bit DAC on SPI chip select CS0. Its analog output drives Q4, which changes the **CURRENT-ADJUST** node at U2’s adjust input. U2 derives an adjustable rail from VIR. This rail biases Q1’s gate and supplies U3, so the DAC affects the attainable pulse current through gate bias. |
| Flash input and gate drive · sheet 4 | D4, R8 1 kΩ, U3 MCP1407, Q3, D5 | DIAG reaches the **GATE-DRV** node through D4. R8 pulls the node low at idle. U3 is a non-inverting MOSFET gate driver, which switches Q2 using the adjustable rail. Q3 switches the D5 strobe indicator in response to GATE-DRV. The alternate MOSI-to-strobe path through R20 and D3 is marked unpopulated on this sheet. |
| Switched LED return and current measurement · sheet 4 | Q1 and Q2 IRLU024NPBF, R10 0.1 Ω | During a flash, current flows from VIR through the emitter board, then through VIR-RTN, Q1, Q2, and R10 to ground. Q1’s gate is biased by the adjustable rail and Q2 enables the path during the pulse. R10 turns the total return current into a voltage at **CUR-SENSE**. The schematic does not show a separate analog feedback loop that senses each string’s current. |
| Digital readback · sheet 6 | U6 MCP3202, R21 2 kΩ, R22 1 kΩ | U6 is a 12-bit ADC on CS1. CH0 reads CUR-SENSE, so total current is inferred from the shunt voltage divided by 0.1 Ω. CH1 reads **LDO-SENSE**. The divider formed by R21 and R22 feeds about one-third of U2’s output to that channel. The ADC gives the controller a way to check the gate-drive rail and total pulse current. It cannot tell how much current each LED string carried. |

The useful voltage checkpoints are **TP1** for 5 V input, **TP2** for ground, **TP3** for U2’s adjustable rail, **TP4** for the current-sense voltage, **TP5** for the strobe input to U3, and **TP6** for the DAC output. The boost converter’s supply rating and the strobe’s pulse current describe different parts of the circuit. A short flash draws stored energy from C6, while the boost converter replenishes that energy over a longer interval. Schematic pulse annotations are design references. Operating current, pulse width, and repetition limits depend on the installed components and measured waveforms. ## IRLED2 emitter board

Sheet 7 shows ten Vishay **VSMA1085400** 850 nm emitters. D6–D10 form one series string and D11–D15 form a second. The strings share a supply node and a return node. Each string therefore sees the same voltage across its five emitters. The sheet shows no individual current-balancing resistors or separate sensing for either branch. Differences in forward voltage and temperature can affect how the strings share current, even when R10 reports the expected total.

The emitter PCB uses surface-mount assembly. Placement and inspection should check emitter polarity, solder joints, and thermal-pad attachment. The V3 driver board uses through-hole components. Its assembly checks instead focus on installed values, part orientation, the matching population list, and the supply and return connections between boards. Use the fabrication and assembly files for the matching PCB revision to identify physical pads and connector orientation. ## Fairway Fusion integration questions

Our planned Pico-controlled setup needs to preserve the distinction between the SPI configuration path, the strobe trigger, and the high-current LED return. We reviewed the **MC34063ABN** substitution for U1 and the **TC4420CPA** substitution for U3. Those substitutions still need verification in the assembled circuit. The separate camera-trigger buffer serves the camera interface. After board bring-up, the measurements needed for synchronized capture are the actual exposure overlap and optical flash timing, tied to the Pico timing report and capture ID.
